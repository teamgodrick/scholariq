# Effective Study Skills

Active recall means testing yourself instead of rereading; it builds stronger memory than passive review. Spaced repetition reviews material at growing intervals, such as 1, 3, 7 and 14 days, so you revisit facts just before you would forget them. Interleaving mixes different topics or problem types in one session, which improves the ability to choose the right method.

The Feynman technique means explaining a concept in plain words as if teaching a beginner; the gaps in your explanation show what you still need to learn. The Pomodoro technique uses 25 minutes of focused work followed by a 5-minute break, with a longer break after four sessions. The 20-20-20 rule for eye comfort says every 20 minutes look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds.

Exam strategy: start revision early, list the syllabus topics, and plan days in advance with the final day for quick revision of weak areas. Practise with past papers under timed conditions. Prioritise by marks weightage and by how weak you are in a topic. Sleep matters for memory consolidation, so avoid all-night cramming and aim for regular sleep. This is general study advice, not medical advice.

When overwhelmed, break work into the smallest next step, keep a short daily list of two or three must-do tasks, and review the list each evening. Track habits with streaks to build consistency, and reward yourself after finishing a block of work.
